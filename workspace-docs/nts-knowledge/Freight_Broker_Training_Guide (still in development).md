# Freight Broker Training Guide: Oversize & Equipment Transport

# **1\. Understand the Niche**

Oversize and heavy equipment transport isn't your average freight. It involves:  
\- Specialized Equipment: Lowboys, flatbeds, step decks, and RGN trailers.  
\- Permits & Routing: State-specific oversize/overweight permits, route planning, and escort vehicles.  
\- Insurance: Higher liability and cargo insurance due to the value and risk involved.  
\- Regulations: Compliance with FMCSA, DOT, and state-specific laws.

# **2\. Get Trained**

While there are free resources, specialized training is crucial for this niche.  
\- LearnFreight: Offers a comprehensive course with access to TMS software and discounted load boards. (learnfreight.com)  
\- DAT & Freight 360: Provides industry-recognized training with mentorship and tools. (dat.com)  
\- Boss Dispatching Academy: Covers dispatching and trucking, including contract training and load board navigation. (thebossdispatchingacademy.com)

# **3\. Obtain Necessary Licenses**

Required licenses and certifications include:  
\- FMCSA Broker Authority (MC Number): Required to legally operate as a freight broker.  
\- Surety Bond (BMC-84): A $75,000 bond to ensure financial responsibility.  
\- Process Agent (BOC-3): Designates a legal agent in each state where you operate.  
\- Insurance: While not always mandatory, it's advisable to have liability and cargo insurance.

# **4\. Build a Network**

\- Shippers: Manufacturers, construction companies, and equipment dealers.  
\- Carriers: Specialized carriers with equipment for oversize loads.  
\- Industry Contacts: Join associations like the Transportation Intermediaries Association (TIA) for networking and resources.

# **5\. Utilize Load Boards**

\- DAT Load Board: Offers access to a vast network of loads and carriers.  
\- TruckStop: Another reputable load board with a focus on oversize and heavy haul.

# **6\. Understand the Logistics**

\- Route Planning: Know legal height, weight, and width restrictions.  
\- Escort Vehicles: Determine when they're required based on load dimensions.  
\- Permits: Understand the process and costs associated with obtaining state-specific permits.

# **7\. Stay Compliant**

Regularly review and stay updated on:  
\- FMCSA Regulations: Ensure adherence to federal rules.  
\- State Laws: Each state may have unique requirements for oversize loads.  
\- Insurance Policies: Regularly review to ensure adequate coverage.
